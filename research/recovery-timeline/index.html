Hair transplant recovery, day by day

Published 17 November 2026. A diary kept day by day, not reconstructed afterwards.

Nearly every recovery timeline online is published by a clinic that sells the operation. This one is a diary. I wrote down what my own head looked like, from the morning of surgery to five months later: one session, about 5,000 grafts, FUE. It is the same transplant described on the About page. It is a single case. Read it as a shape, not as a schedule.

The five stretches you actually live through

Days 0 to 7, swelling and crusts. The swollen face, not the scalp, is what makes the first week awkward. It peaked on day 2 and was the only part that looked alarming. Headaches ran to about day 6.

Days 7 to 14, back to normal under a cap. Public places were comfortable from day 9, a long flight on day 14 caused no trouble, and the last crusts washed off on day 13.

Days 21 to 30, the shed. The transplanted hairs fall out. On day 28 the recipient area was bald again, which is the one stage that looks like a failed operation and is not. Spots and inflamed bumps followed around day 37.

Month 2, nothing visible. Growth restarted around day 48 but stayed a thin fuzz for weeks. The redness finally went at about day 62.

Months 3 to 5, the result. Growth accelerated at day 86, coverage evened out by day 95, and by day 114 nothing gave the operation away. The hairstyle was fully back on day 125.

Where this case is typical, and where it is not

Published timelines put the start of shedding at two to four weeks, peaking around week six. Day 24 here sits inside that. The rest of this log runs fast. First new hairs usually appear in months three to four; here they showed at day 48. A result that reads as a normal head of hair at four months is quicker than most people get, and the ISHRS position is that the final result should not be judged before twelve months, with the crown taking up to eighteen.

Two honest caveats. Most of the "typical" numbers circulating online come from clinic marketing pages rather than dermatology journals, so they are soft. And this is one person with one surgeon, one graft count and one scalp. If your month two looks like nothing is happening, that matches both this log and the published ranges.

Five things that make recovery go better

Start from what is actually under your control. Nothing on this list makes hair grow faster, because the follicles set that pace. What you can change is whether you lose grafts in the first two weeks and how long the visible stages drag on.

  1. Stop smoking, and leave alcohol alone around the surgery strongest evidence of the five

    Nicotine narrows exactly the vessels that have to feed the new grafts. A study of patients with androgenic alopecia found smokers and drinkers had worse transplant outcomes, and smokers bled more at the graft sites. General surgical advice from the American College of Surgeons is to stop four to six weeks before an operation and stay off cigarettes for four weeks after.

  2. Sleep with your head raised for the first three or four nights clinic protocol, not trials

    Swelling peaked on day 2 in this log and was gone inside a week. Sleeping semi-upright and holding a cold compress on the forehead, never on the grafts, is the standard instruction for keeping it short.

  3. Wash exactly as the clinic told you, and never pick a crust protocol, with a clear failure mode

    The crusts came off by themselves on day 13 here. Pulling one off early can bring the graft with it, and a graft lost in the first two weeks does not grow back.

  4. Keep sweat, sun and pressure off the scalp for two to four weeks protocol

    That means no gym, sauna or pool, and no tight hat sitting on the grafts; a loose cap is fine, and was worn from day 5 in this log. Sun on healing skin keeps the redness around for longer.

  5. Fix the boring things: sleep, protein, iron weak evidence, cheap to do

    None of this makes a follicle grow faster. But low iron and low vitamin D are known causes of diffuse shedding, so a blood test is worth it if you shed heavily. Do not start or stop finasteride or minoxidil around the surgery without asking the surgeon who operated on you.

Your clinic's written aftercare beats anything on this page, and this page is not medical advice. If you were not given written instructions before flying home, that is itself a warning sign, and it is one of the 15 things to check before you book.

Method

One patient, one FUE session of about 5,000 grafts, notes written on the day rather than from memory. We converted the calendar dates to days since surgery and removed place names; no photographs are published. Nothing here was supplied, reviewed or paid for by a clinic.
